01 — Sales Philosophy

How RMS sells —
and how we don't.

Six principles that govern every sales conversation. If a pitch, email, or proposal violates one of these, it does not go out. Junior salespeople should be able to recite all six from memory in their first week.

// 01
Consultative, not transactional

We diagnose before we prescribe. Every discovery call starts with "what are you currently running" and ends with the prospect telling us their pain — not us pitching features.

// 02
Value-led, not discount-led

We never open with a discount. We open with the credential trio and the outcome. Discounting before the prospect anchors on value tells them the price was inflated to begin with.

// 03
MD-attended onboarding

Every retainer client meets the MD personally at kickoff. Senior accountability is a sales differentiator — and a retention insurance policy worth more than any SLA clause.

// 04
One conversation, one number

We sell the whole portfolio in every meeting. The SME buying laptops is being mentally pre-qualified for M365, NDPR, and managed IT. Cross-engine bundling triples lifetime value.

// 05
Transparent pricing

Our website publishes Naira pricing. We do not play games. The prospect arrives at the call already qualified on price — every minute saved on price negotiation is a minute spent on fit.

// 06
Velocity over volume

A 3-week SME cycle from first call to MSA is the standard. Speed is a feature — the prospect who waited four months at an incumbent integrator is the prospect who signs with RMS in twelve days.

03 — Where Prospects Come From

Four sources.
Five hours per day.

Prospect generation is a daily discipline, not an occasional campaign. The BizDev Lead spends 5 hours every day across these four sources. No exceptions, no "I'll catch up next week."

LinkedIn outbound
// 2 hrs/day

The primary engine. We connect with founders, COOs, CTOs, and IT managers at Lagos SMEs and corporates. Personalised connection request, then a no-pitch nurture message, then a soft demo invite at week 2.

  • Daily target: 30 personalised connection requests
  • Sales Navigator filters: Lagos · 10–500 staff · Founder/COO/CTO/IT
  • Auto-decline anyone outside Nigeria or below 10 staff
  • Weekly LinkedIn post from CEO — "M365 in plain English" series
Cold email sequences
// 1.5 hrs/day

Sourced lists from Apollo, ZoomInfo, or LinkedIn export. 4-touch sequence over 21 days. Stop sequence the moment a prospect replies — even with "not interested."

  • Daily target: 50 new prospects added to sequence
  • Reply rate target: 8%+ on first touch
  • Meeting booking rate target: 2.5% of contacts
  • Domain rotation: 2nd domain (mail.rmstech.ng) for sequenced sends
Partner channel
// 1 hr/day

TD Africa referrals, Microsoft co-sell, accountant/lawyer referral partners. The highest-converting source — partner-referred prospects close at 2–3× the rate of cold outbound.

  • Monthly check-in with TD Africa channel manager
  • Quarterly co-marketing with 1–2 accounting firms
  • Microsoft Partner Center: register all deals for co-sell
  • Referral fee: 5% of first-year revenue to introducer
Events & community
// 0.5 hr/day + 2 events/mo

Lagos tech meetups, CIBN/CITN events, NDPR/cybersecurity webinars, Microsoft community days. Slow-burn but builds the brand and produces inbound over time.

  • Attend 2 industry events monthly minimum
  • Host one webinar per quarter (NDPR or Copilot themed)
  • Sponsor one industry event Y1 for credibility
  • Daily: 30 minutes scanning industry news for trigger events

05 — Discovery Call Framework

Twenty minutes.
One outcome.

The discovery call has exactly one objective: end the call with either a booked demo or a "no, not for us." Anything else is a deferred decision dressed up as progress. Stick to 20 minutes — long discovery loses prospects.

20-Minute Discovery Call · Standard Flow
Hard cap: 22 min
0–2 min
Open · rapport + agenda lock

Thank them for the time. Confirm you have 20 minutes. State the agenda: "I'll ask a few questions about your current setup, share what we typically do for companies your size, and at the end we'll either book a proper demo or you'll tell me it's not a fit. Sound fair?"

2–14 min
Discover · twelve questions, in order

Listen for: pain, urgency, budget signal, decision process. Take notes. Resist the urge to pitch in this section — every minute you spend listening compounds the close.

  • Walk me through your current Microsoft setup — what plan are people on?
  • How many staff use it daily? Any contractors or external users?
  • Who handles IT today — internal team, freelancer, or somebody's nephew?
  • When something breaks, what's the typical resolution time?
  • What hardware are people using — company laptops or BYOD?
  • Last time you bought laptops, who did you buy from?
  • Any recent security concerns or breach scares?
  • Where does your client data live — OneDrive, local drives, third-party?
  • Have you done anything specific for NDPR compliance?
  • What's prompting you to look at this now — what's changing?
  • Who else would be involved in a decision like this?
  • If we could fix all of that, what would it be worth to you?
14–18 min
Reframe · position the next step

Reflect back two or three of the pains they mentioned. Then position which RMS package fits — by name. "Based on what you've described, you're a fit for RMS Business — that's M365 Business Standard plus managed IT essentials. For 25 users that lands at roughly ₦340k/month all-in." Mention TD Africa for hardware if they raised it. Mention NDPR if compliance came up.

18–20 min
Close · book the demo or close the loop

"Two ways we usually take this forward: a 45-minute proper demo where I show you exactly what your environment would look like, or I send you a written proposal off this call and you decide on your own time. Which works better?" Lock the calendar invite live, on the call. If they hedge, ask directly: "Is this a no for now, or a not-yet?" A clean no is more valuable than a soft maybe.

06 — Objection Handling

The ten
most common objections.

If a prospect raises any of these, you respond in your own words — but the response below is the spine. Practise out loud until each lands in under 45 seconds. Long objection responses signal weakness.

"It's too expensive."
"Compared to what? If it's compared to your current Microsoft direct spend, our CSP pricing is actually cheaper at your headcount. If it's compared to having no IT support — yes, paying for IT is more expensive than not paying for it, until something breaks and you spend two weeks recovering. Which comparison are we making?"
"We already use Google Workspace."
"Got it. A lot of companies stay on Google long-term and that's fine. The conversations where it makes sense to switch are usually about Excel-heavy finance teams, Microsoft Teams for enterprise clients who use it, or Copilot for AI productivity. Are any of those landing for you? If not, no switch needed — but we also do hardware and IT support regardless of which email you use."
"We buy our M365 direct from Microsoft."
"That's normal — most growing companies start there. Two things buying direct doesn't get you: better-than-direct pricing (CSP partners price below Microsoft direct in most cases), and provisioning support (we configure MFA, Defender, Intune, NDPR — Microsoft direct just gives you the licence). Want me to show you the side-by-side?"
"We have a guy who does our IT."
"Fantastic — we work alongside internal IT all the time. Two questions: does your guy hold Microsoft certifications, and what happens when he's on leave or unavailable? Most clients keep their internal person for day-to-day and use us for the heavy lifting — licensing, security, hardware procurement, and as backup coverage."
"Just send me a proposal."
"Happy to. To make sure the proposal is actually useful and not a generic template, I need 15 minutes to understand your headcount, what you're running today, and what you're trying to fix. Otherwise I send you a 12-page document and you spend an hour figuring out which 3 pages apply. Can we do the 15 minutes Thursday morning, then you have the proposal Friday?"
"We need to compare with two other vendors."
"Completely reasonable. To make the comparison fair, ask the other two: are they a Microsoft CSP partner, do they have a published Naira pricing schedule, and who specifically signs off on retention. If the answers are yes/yes/MD, we're comparing apples to apples. If not, we're not. I'm happy to come back after you've spoken to them."
"What if you go out of business?"
"Fair question for any IT partner. Two facts: your Microsoft tenant belongs to you, not to us — if we vanished tomorrow, you'd contact any other Microsoft CSP partner and transfer in 48 hours. And your hardware is sourced from TD Africa, who has been distributing Microsoft and HP across West Africa since 1999. The infrastructure you're buying is durable independent of us."
"What about uptime — what if Microsoft goes down?"
"Microsoft 365 has a 99.9% uptime SLA — financially backed. That works out to roughly 8 hours of downtime per year in the absolute worst case. In practice over the last 3 years it's been measurably better. Compare to typical on-premise email servers in Lagos — power, internet, hardware — most companies see 8+ hours of downtime per month on local setups."
"What about NDPR — we don't really do that."
"You should — NITDA is actively enforcing, and fines are up to 2% of annual gross revenue or ₦10 million, whichever is higher. The good news: NDPR compliance is included in our deployment by default. You don't need to do anything beyond signing the documentation we provide. We treat it as table stakes, not a separate sales line."
"We'll think about it / get back to you."
"Of course. To make sure I don't pester you — is there something specific holding it up, or is the timing just not right? If there's a specific concern, I'd rather address it now; if it's timing, just tell me roughly when to circle back. I'd rather get a clean 'not now' than spend the next month guessing."
07 — Proposal Template

Every proposal,
eight sections.

In this order. The structure is fixed; the content is tailored. A new BizDev hire should be able to assemble a proposal in 90 minutes by working through these eight sections in sequence.

01
Cover page

Client name, proposal title, RMS Technologies branding, date, validity (30 days), and the credential trio in the footer. No prose on this page.

02
Our understanding of your need

Three to four sentences reflecting back exactly what the prospect told you in discovery. This page proves we listened and is the single most important page in the document.

03
Recommended package

Name the package (RMS Starter / Business / Professional / Enterprise / NGO-Edu / Copilot Add-On). State what's included as a clean checklist. No fluff, no buzzwords.

04
Pricing

Naira pricing, all-inclusive monthly cost, plus a one-time setup fee if applicable. Show the per-user cost and the total. Annual billing option with 8% discount footnote. VAT clearly labelled.

05
Implementation timeline

Day-by-day for a typical 5-day deployment, or week-by-week for larger engagements. Lists who does what (RMS vs client). Always specify a go-live date — a date in the document is a commitment, not a hope.

06
SLA commitments

Response times, uptime guarantee, escalation path, support hours. The MD's direct line for the named senior contact above ₦1m monthly spend.

07
Pre-engagement actions

What we need from the client before kickoff: list of users, current domain admin access, signed DPA, payment method confirmed. Sequence matters — the proposal converts only if the client knows what they need to do next.

08
About RMS Technologies

Half a page. The one-liner, the credential trio, two named partnerships (Microsoft CSP, TD Africa), the registered company name and RC number, and a single sentence on the MD's senior accountability commitment.
